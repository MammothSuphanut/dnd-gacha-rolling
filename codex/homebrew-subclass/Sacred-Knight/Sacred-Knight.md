# Sacred Knight

*A warrior who looks inward before looking outward. The deeper a Sacred Knight understands their own convictions, the more of their inner Fervor they can call upon — a burning devotion that sharpens a blade, steadies a shield, and refuses to bend. This power answers only to the disciplined and the Lawful; it has no interest in the lawless or the uncommitted.*

## Designer's Note

The Sacred Knight draws its inspiration from the Knight orders of the Chinese novel/donghua *Throne of Seal* (神印王座 / *Shen Yin Wang Zuo*), which splits its knights into Guardian Knights (守护骑士, defense and support) and Retribution Knights (惩戒骑士, offense and punishment). Several technique names in this document are translated or adapted directly from techniques used in the source material.

The class was built around two experiments: giving a martial class a resource pool that looks exactly like a caster's spell slots but can never be used to cast a spell, and tying that resource's very availability to a character's alignment rather than treating alignment as pure flavor. Neither experiment has an exact precedent in official 5e design.

## An Oath Bound to Law

A Sacred Knight's power is not granted by a god, a pact, or a bloodline — it rises from within, and it answers only to a mind at peace with its own convictions. Mechanically, that means a Sacred Knight **must be Lawful** (Lawful Good, Lawful Neutral, or Lawful Evil) to use any feature tied to Fervor: Fervor Awakening, every Fervor Technique, and everything else keyed to the word "Fervor" in this document. Features that don't involve Fervor — ordinary weapon attacks, proficiencies, saving throws, and so on — always function normally regardless of alignment.

If your alignment stops being Lawful — through your own choices, a spell or effect that forces an alignment change, or your GM's judgment that your sustained behavior has genuinely shifted your convictions — **every Fervor feature you have shuts off immediately** until you become Lawful again. A single unlawful or dishonorable act doesn't cost you this power on its own; your GM should distinguish between a lapse (you're still Lawful) and a true change of heart (you lose access to Fervor). Any Fervor you're carrying when this happens isn't lost — it simply can't be spent until you're Lawful again.

### The Ten Tenets

The tenets below, drawn from the knightly codes of *Throne of Seal*, aren't a mechanical checklist — they're the yardstick a GM should use when judging whether a lapse is temporary or a true break from Law.

1. **Humility.** Do not exalt yourself above others, or use your power and station to belittle those beneath you. Own your failures honestly.
2. **Honesty.** Do not deceive others for personal gain. Let your word and your deed be one.
3. **Compassion.** Show mercy to the weak. Do not strike down those who yield or cannot defend themselves without true cause.
4. **Valor.** Do not abandon your duty out of fear. Face danger to protect what deserves protecting.
5. **Justice.** Judge and punish by the truth of the deed alone — never by whim or personal vengeance.
6. **Sacrifice.** When it truly matters, give up your own safety or gain for the sake of others.
7. **Honor.** Keep your word. Do not resort to treachery, or betray those who trust you.
8. **Perseverance.** Do not abandon your duty or your convictions for hardship's sake. Stand until the task is done.
9. **Benevolence.** Grant a chance to atone when one is warranted. Do not condemn or destroy beyond what a situation demands.
10. **Righteousness.** Hold to what is right, even against the orders of those above you or against your own interest.

## Fervor

Every Sacred Knight carries a table of spell slots identical to a full spellcaster's (see the Sacred Knight table below) — but **you can never cast a spell with them.** Even if you gain spells from another source while you have levels in this class — a feat like Magic Initiate, a multiclass level in a spellcasting class, or anything else — you cannot expend a Sacred Knight spell slot to cast a spell. These slots exist for one purpose only: converting into Fervor.

**Converting a Spell Slot into Fervor.** As a bonus action, you can convert up to 1 spell slot into Fervor, gaining Fervor equal to **the slot's level + your Wisdom modifier**. (For example, converting a 3rd-level slot with a +4 Wisdom modifier grants 7 Fervor.) This 1-slot cap becomes 2 slots automatically at 18th level (see *Fervor Overflow*); before then, you can temporarily push it to 2 slots in a single bonus action only through *Fervor Surge* (see 2nd level). Converting more than one slot at once still applies your Wisdom modifier separately to each slot converted. This bonus action is the only way to convert a slot into Fervor — there's no option to spend your action instead, so your action remains free to attack or use a Fervor Technique every turn.

**Spending Fervor.** Fervor you've converted stays available to spend immediately or on a later turn. There's no limit on how many Fervor Techniques you can use in a turn — you're limited only by how much Fervor you have to spend.

**Fervor Fades.** Unspent Fervor lasts for 1 minute (10 rounds) after you gain it, then disperses. This holds true at every level, including 20th (see *Transcendent Fervor* for what 20th level changes instead).

**Paying Before You Know the Result.** For any Fervor Technique that affects an attack (such as Faith Halo, Light Sword, or Pure White Blade), you must declare and spend the Fervor **before you make the attack roll.** You can't wait to see whether the attack hits and then decide. This is deliberate: because you never gain Extra Attack (see 5th level), a single missed attack already costs you more than it would most martial characters, and this class accepts that risk rather than removing it.

**Critical Hits.** Damage dice granted by a Fervor Technique (such as Light Sword's extra dice) are doubled on a critical hit, exactly like any other extra damage dice.

**Multiclassing and Spellcasting.** If you have levels in Sacred Knight and also gain levels in a class that grants spellcasting, you still cannot cast spells using spell slots — from any class — for as long as you have at least 1 level of Sacred Knight. This overrides the normal multiclass spellcasting rules; a Sacred Knight 1 / Cleric 19 character cannot cast spells with a spell slot any more than a Sacred Knight 20 character can.

## Class Features

As a Sacred Knight, you gain the following class features.

### 1st Level: Choose Your Path

*Before you learn to awaken Fervor, you choose how you'll carry yourself into battle.*

Choose one of three Paths: **Retribution**, **Guardian**, or **Formless.** This choice is separate from your Sacred Throne, which you choose at 3rd level. Your Path grants additional proficiencies, a signature stance feature, and determines which Fervor Techniques you're able to learn (see *Fervor Techniques*, below).

**Retribution.** A knight of punishment, built around offense and counterattack. You gain the **Twin Blade Stance** feature:

- You can wield one Versatile weapon in each hand at the same time (a pair of longswords, a pair of warhammers, and so on). This isn't governed by the two-weapon fighting rules in any way — you don't gain an extra attack with your bonus action from doing this.
- Any Versatile weapon you wield uses its two-handed damage die at all times, whether you're wielding it in one hand or two (a longsword deals 1d10 instead of 1d8, for example).
- You gain a +1 bonus to AC while wielding two weapons this way.
- You can draw or sheathe two weapons at once, instead of the usual one.

You can learn Fervor Techniques from the Universal and Retribution lists only.

**Guardian.** A knight of protection, built around defense and supporting allies. You gain proficiency with **Heavy armor** and **Shields**, and you gain the **Bulwark Stance** feature: while you're wielding a shield, you gain an additional +1 bonus to AC (stacking with the shield's normal +2, for a total of +3 AC from the shield).

You can learn Fervor Techniques from the Universal and Guardian lists only.

**Formless.** A knight who refuses to commit to a single stance, shifting between offense and defense as the fight demands. You gain proficiency with **Shields** only (not Heavy armor — you remain limited to Light and Medium armor). You gain the **Adaptive Stance** feature:

- You can swap your off-hand between a weapon and a shield as part of making an attack, or as a **reaction** — including on a turn that isn't your own. This costs no Fervor.
- While wielding two weapons (no shield), your primary weapon uses its two-handed damage die even in one hand, and you gain a +1 bonus to AC — identical to Twin Blade Stance.
- While wielding a shield, you gain only the shield's normal benefit (+2 AC) — it doesn't stack with the two-handed damage die or the +1 AC bonus above, to prevent getting the benefits of both stances at once.

You can learn Fervor Techniques from the Universal, Retribution, and Guardian lists — all three. This is Formless's defining strength, in exchange for never reaching either specialist's peak AC or peak damage.

*Learning versus using a technique.* The list you can learn from is fixed by your Path and never changes — but once you've learned a technique, you can use it at any time, regardless of what you're currently wielding. A Retribution Knight can use a Retribution technique while fighting unarmed, and a Guardian can use a Guardian technique without a shield in hand. Equipment (Twin Blade Stance, Bulwark Stance, and so on) still governs your stance features and armor benefits — it just no longer gates which techniques you're allowed to trigger.

### 1st Level: Fervor Awakening

You've learned to draw Fervor out of yourself. See *Fervor*, above, for the full rules — including the conversion formula (**spell slot level + Wisdom modifier**), which is active starting at this level.

### 1st Level: Fervor Mastery

You learn your first **Fervor Technique**, chosen from the lists your Path allows (see *Choose Your Path* and *Fervor Techniques*).

You gain this feature again at 2nd, 5th, 9th, and 13th level, learning one new technique each time (5 techniques total across the whole class).

Fervor Techniques don't have a rank or upgrade track — each technique's numbers are fixed for the whole game, whatever level you learn it at. Your power grows through the Fervor you have available and how quickly you can convert it (see *Fervor Surge*, *Fervor Surge Improvement*, and *Fervor Overflow*) and through learning more techniques, not by upgrading the techniques you already know.

### 2nd Level: Fervor Surge

Once per short rest, when you take the bonus action to convert a spell slot into Fervor, you can spend a use of Fervor Surge to **convert one additional slot** as part of the same bonus action (bringing the total to 2 slots converted in that bonus action, or 3 once you have Fervor Overflow at 18th level). This doesn't cost an extra action, since it rides along with the bonus action you're already using. Your Wisdom modifier applies separately to the additional slot, same as any other slot you convert.

You can use Fervor Surge only **once per turn**, no matter how many uses per short rest you have available — extra uses let you open a burst like this on more turns per short rest, not more than once in the same turn.

This is meant as an opening tool: use it at the start of a fight to convert two slots in a single burst instead of building up Fervor over several turns, addressing the "weak first turn" problem of a class with no Extra Attack.

### 3rd Level: Sacred Throne

You pledge yourself to one of the six Sacred Thrones, gaining features at 3rd level and again at 6th, 10th, 14th, and 17th level. See the individual Sacred Throne documents for the features each one grants.

### 4th Level: Ability Score Improvement

You gain this feature, as normal, again at 8th, 12th, 16th, and 19th level.

### 5th Level: Fervor Mastery

You learn your third Fervor Technique.

*You never gain Extra Attack, at this level or any other* — unusual for a martial class, and the trade-off this class is built around. Most of your damage comes from Fervor Techniques instead, and gaining a new technique at this exact level is meant to directly offset the level where other martial classes typically pull ahead.

### 6th Level: Sacred Throne Feature

You gain a feature from your Sacred Throne. See its document for details.

### 7th Level: Improve Fervor Technique (I)

Choose one Fervor Technique you know (from the Universal, Retribution, or Guardian lists, or one granted by your Sacred Throne) and upgrade it permanently. Depending on the technique, this either grants it a new capability or improves its Fervor exchange rate — see each technique's entry under *Fervor Techniques* for its specific improvement.

You gain this feature again at 15th level (see *Improve Fervor Technique (II)*). You must choose a **different** technique each time — you can never improve the same technique twice.

### 18th Level: Fervor Overflow

Converting spell slots into Fervor becomes more efficient — your bonus action can now convert **2 slots at once** automatically, every turn, up from 1 (this is the only permanent increase to your bonus-action cap across the whole class; your Wisdom modifier still applies separately to each slot). *Fervor Surge* still functions after this level, pushing you to **3 slots** in the bonus action you use it (still limited to once per turn, and to your uses per short rest from *Fervor Surge Improvement*).

### 20th Level: Transcendent Fervor

Once per long rest, when you take a short rest, you can choose to have that short rest **restore all of your spell slots to their maximum**, exactly as a long rest would — solely for the purpose of converting them into Fervor. This doesn't restore hit points, Hit Dice, or any other benefit tied to a long rest, and you can use it only once between long rests.

The rule that Fervor fades after 1 minute (10 rounds) still applies without exception, even at 20th level. This capstone exists to solve the "burned out by the second fight of the day" problem, giving you a second full day's worth of Fervor once per long rest — not to let Fervor stockpile indefinitely across fights or days.

## Fervor Techniques

Each Fervor Technique has a fixed **Cost** — the amount of Fervor you spend to use it. There's no rank or upgrade track beyond what *Improve Fervor Technique* grants; the numbers below are final for the whole game. This is a starter list, meant to be expanded over time.

Any technique you've learned can be used at any time, regardless of what you're currently wielding — Fervor Techniques are no longer gated by equipment. Which techniques you're able to *learn* is fixed by your Path (see *Choose Your Path*): a Retribution Knight can learn from the Universal and Retribution lists, a Guardian from the Universal and Guardian lists, and a Formless Knight from all three.

**Using multiple techniques on a multi-attack turn.** A technique that grants multiple attacks in a single turn (such as Cross Slash) doesn't automatically apply another attack-linked technique (such as Faith Halo or Light Sword) to every one of those attacks. If you want an effect like that to apply to each attack individually, you must pay that technique's Fervor cost separately for each attack.

### Universal Techniques

**Pure White Blade.** *Cost: 1 Fervor per stack.* When you make an attack, you can increase its range by 5 feet per stack of Fervor spent, usable on both melee and ranged attacks. If you spend 5 or more Fervor on a single use (25 feet or more of bonus range), you also gain advantage on that attack roll. You can spend a maximum of your Wisdom modifier + Proficiency Bonus in stacks on a single use.

*Improvement (7th or 15th level).* Each stack now grants +10 feet of range instead of +5 (the cost and stack cap don't change).

**Swift.** *Cost: 1 Fervor per stack.* As a free action, usable once per turn, you increase your Speed by 10 feet per stack of Fervor spent until the start of your next turn, and you ignore difficult terrain for as long as the effect lasts. The bonus Speed from this technique is calculated separately from your base Speed — Dash only doubles your base Speed, not this bonus. You can spend a maximum of your Wisdom modifier + Proficiency Bonus in stacks on a single use.

*Improvement (7th or 15th level).* Each stack now grants +15 feet of Speed instead of +10 (the cost, duration, and stack cap don't change).

**Faith Halo.** *Cost: 1 Fervor per stack.* When you make an attack, you increase the attack roll by +1 per stack of Fervor spent (paid before you roll — see *Paying Before You Know the Result*). You can spend a maximum of your Wisdom modifier + Proficiency Bonus in stacks on a single use.

*Improvement (7th or 15th level).* For every 2 additional Fervor you spend beyond the base 1-per-+1 rate, you gain an extra +1 to the attack roll. The stack cap doesn't change.

**Clarity.** *Cost: 1 Fervor per stack.*As a Reaction action, Before making any ability check of your own, you can spend Fervor to add +1 per stack to the check. You must declare this before you roll — you can't apply it after seeing the result. You can spend a maximum of your Wisdom modifier + Proficiency Bonus in stacks on a single check.

*Improvement (7th or 15th level).* For every 2 additional Fervor you spend beyond the base rate, you gain an extra +1 to the check. The stack cap doesn't change.

### Retribution Techniques

**Rising Dragon Strike.** *Cost: 3 Fervor.* When you're hit by an attack, you can spend 3 Fervor to immediately make one counterattack with a weapon you're wielding. This doesn't cost your reaction. You can use this every time you're hit — it's limited only by your available Fervor, not by a per-turn cap, since how often you're hit is already a natural limiter.

*Improvement (7th or 15th level).* The cost drops from 3 Fervor to **2 Fervor** per use.

**Light Sword.** *Cost: 1 Fervor per stack.* When you make an attack, you add 1d4 radiant damage per stack of Fervor spent. You can spend a maximum of your Wisdom modifier + Proficiency Bonus in stacks on a single attack.

*Improvement (7th or 15th level).* Each stack now deals 1d6 radiant damage instead of 1d4 (the number of dice and the stack cap don't change).

### Guardian Techniques

**Shared Reaction.** Spirit Ward, Guardian's Blessing, and Bulwark Shift all use the same reaction — one per round, as normal. However, a single use of your reaction can trigger **all three** of these techniques against the same triggering event at once, as long as you have enough Fervor to pay for each one you want to use — you don't have to pick only one. (For example, if you fail a saving throw against an effect that also deals damage, you can spend your one reaction to use both Guardian's Blessing, to turn the failed save into a success, and Spirit Ward, to reduce the damage, in the same reaction — as long as you can afford both costs.) A reaction granted by another feature (such as Vanguard's Instinct) is a separate reaction and isn't affected by this rule.

**Spirit Ward.** *Cost: 1 Fervor reduces damage by 2.* When you're hit by an attack and know the damage, you can spend Fervor to reduce that damage at a rate of 1 Fervor per 2 points of damage (for example, spending 5 Fervor against 10 damage negates it entirely). Spending less still reduces damage proportionally. (See *Shared Reaction*, above.) You can spend a maximum of your Wisdom modifier + Proficiency Bonus in stacks on a single reduction.

*Improvement (7th or 15th level).* The rate improves to a flat **1 Fervor per 3 points of damage**.

**Guardian's Blessing.** *Cost: 2 Fervor per point below the DC.* When you fail a saving throw, you can spend Fervor immediately after learning the result to turn that failure into a success, at a rate of 2 Fervor per point you fell short of the DC (for example, rolling a 10 against DC 15 — 5 points short — costs 10 Fervor to succeed). (See *Shared Reaction*, above.) You can cover a maximum of your Wisdom modifier + Proficiency Bonus points below the DC on a single use.

*Improvement (7th or 15th level).* The rate improves to a flat **1 Fervor per point**.

**Bulwark Shift.** *Cost: 1 Fervor per 5 feet (reaction).* You spend Fervor based on distance (1 Fervor per 5 feet) to swap places with one creature — ally or enemy — within the distance you can afford. The swapped creature is moved to a space within 5 feet of its original position (you choose where). This movement doesn't provoke opportunity attacks. (See *Shared Reaction*, above.)

*Improvement (7th or 15th level).* The cost is halved to **1 Fervor per 10 feet**, and the creature you swap can be relocated up to **10 feet** from its original position (up from 5 feet).

## Multiclassing

### Proficiencies Gained

When you gain a level in Sacred Knight other than your first, you gain the following proficiencies: Light armor, Medium armor, Simple weapons, and Martial weapons with the Versatile property.

### Spellcasting

As stated under *Fervor*, above: **at least 1 level of Sacred Knight prevents you from casting spells with a spell slot at all**, regardless of what other classes you have levels in. This is a deliberate exception to the normal multiclass spellcasting rules, not an oversight — confirm this with your table before play.
