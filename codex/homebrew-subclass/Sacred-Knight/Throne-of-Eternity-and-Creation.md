# Throne of Eternity and Creation

*Where the other five Thrones are echoes of a single true seat of power, this one is the origin — the wellspring the rest were copied from. It carries no separate knightly title, because it has nothing to inherit from; it is the thing itself. Its knights don't destroy to create, or create to destroy — they simply understand that nothing is ever truly gone, only remade.*

A Sacred Knight who pledges to the Throne of Eternity and Creation channels Fervor into restoration as readily as into the sword — sustaining allies, healing wounds, and turning a single strike into a wave that reshapes the battlefield around it.

## Sacred Throne Features

### 3rd Level: Everlasting Grace

*A wound is not permanent. Neither is a life, if you refuse to let it end.*

As an action, you can spend Fervor to heal yourself or one ally within 30 feet, restoring hit points equal to 1d4 per Fervor spent. This healing has no stack cap beyond the Fervor you have available.

*Improve Fervor Technique (7th or 15th level).* Everlasting Grace's healing die improves from 1d4 to 1d6 per Fervor spent. This also improves the temporary hit points granted by Undying Providence (10th level).

### 6th Level: Genesis Wave

*Creation was never meant to be aimed at only one thing.*

Whenever you use Pure White Blade, you can spend additional Fervor to turn that attack into an area effect instead of a single-target one, provided your chosen point of origin is within the range Pure White Blade calculated. Spending an additional 5 Fervor causes the attack to strike every creature in a 5-foot square at that point; spending a further 5 Fervor (10 total) expands the area to a 10-foot square.

Once you have your 17th-level Throne feature, you can pay the cost of this combination separately for multiple points of origin within the same action — for example, striking three separate clusters of enemies by paying Pure White Blade's cost three times.

### 10th Level: Undying Providence

*A shield built from the same grace that mends flesh.*

You can spend 1 Fervor per stack — the same rate as Everlasting Grace — to grant yourself or an ally temporary hit points equal to 1d4 per Fervor spent, with no stack cap beyond your available Fervor. If you've upgraded Everlasting Grace through Improve Fervor Technique, this uses 1d6 per stack instead.

When you spend multiple stacks in a single use, roll all the dice together as one pool (for example, spending 10 Fervor means rolling 10d4 at once, not ten separate 1d4 rolls). If you use this feature again as a separate use, it follows the normal rule for temporary hit points — the two amounts don't add together, and the target keeps whichever is higher.

### 14th Level: Absolute Genesis

*To tear the world apart is easy. To remain whole while doing it — that is Creation's real gift.*

If you use Pure White Blade and spend 5 or more total Fervor on that attack (such as by triggering Genesis Wave's area mode), the attack ignores resistance to physical damage, and you gain resistance to physical damage for 1 minute.

### 17th Level: Throne of Eternity and Creation

*The origin remembers what it means to make something from nothing.*

You can activate this Throne by spending spell slots with a combined level of 10 (calculated the same way as converting Fervor, but spent to activate the Throne rather than banked as Fervor). You aren't limited to activating it a fixed number of times per long rest — you're limited only by how many spell slots you have available across the day. Once activated, the Throne's effect lasts for 1 minute.

While active, you can use Pure White Blade or Everlasting Grace against up to a number of separate targets equal to your Wisdom modifier within a single action, paying each technique's Fervor cost separately per target.
